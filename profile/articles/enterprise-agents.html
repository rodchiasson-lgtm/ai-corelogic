Designing agents for the realities of enterprise work

By Rodney Chiasson · Portfolio article

Enterprise agents do not operate in a vacuum. They encounter permissions, sensitive data, business rules, and people accountable for outcomes. The architecture should make those realities explicit from the start.

Bound the authority

Give each agent only the tools and data needed for its assigned task. Separate read and write actions, use scoped identities, and require approval at consequential boundaries. The objective is not to make agents inert; it is to make their authority understandable and proportionate.

Keep decisions reviewable

Record the inputs, tool calls, policy checks, and human interventions that shape an outcome. Logs should help an operator explain what happened without treating a model’s fluent answer as evidence of correctness.

Evaluate the workflow

Test realistic tasks, failure paths, and handoffs—not only isolated prompts. Track quality, safety, latency, and cost, then route uncertain or high-impact cases to accountable reviewers.

Reliable agentic systems emerge when permissions, oversight, and evaluation are part of the design rather than a layer added after a prototype succeeds.
