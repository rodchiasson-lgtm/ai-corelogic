From prototype to production: make the operating model part of the design

By Rodney Chiasson · Portfolio article

Production readiness is often treated as a final gate. That framing misses the point: operating responsibilities shape which architectures are safe and sustainable in the first place.

Design around ownership

Every workflow needs a named owner for its purpose, data access, exceptions, and ongoing review. When responsibilities are implicit, incidents become coordination problems precisely when fast decisions are needed.

Observe the system people actually operate

Monitoring should capture task outcomes, tool failures, policy blocks, response times, and resource use. The view should help teams distinguish model behavior from retrieval, integration, and upstream-data issues.

Plan for change and escalation

Define how prompts, tools, models, and policies are reviewed and released. Provide a clear fallback or human escalation when confidence is inadequate or the task crosses a risk boundary.

These choices are architecture decisions. Bringing them forward makes the path from a useful prototype to a dependable service more deliberate.
